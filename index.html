Disciplines

Four disciplines, one coordinated team

The name Kinetra brings together civil, structural, electrical and mechanical engineering, so a project is planned as one system rather than in separate silos

Civil

Dams, roads, bridges, drainage and site works that prepare the ground for everything built on it

Structural

Analysis and design of concrete and steel structures for gravity, wind and seismic loads

Electrical

Coordination of power infrastructure, from substations and transmission lines to renewable plants

Mechanical

Plant and equipment integration, turbine halls and the structures that support moving machinery

In detail

What each discipline covers

Civil engineering

The ground, water and access that every project depends on. Civil works set the levels, carry the water away and get people and equipment to site.

Typical scope

  • Site grading and earthworks
  • Roads, pavements and access tracks
  • Stormwater drainage and culverts
  • Bridges and river crossings
  • Dams and water-retaining structures
  • Foundation and ground interface

Works closely with

StructuralElectricalMechanical

Structural engineering

The frame that carries every load safely to the ground. This is the core of the practice: 24 years of analysis and design in concrete and steel.

Typical scope

  • Reinforced concrete and structural steel design
  • Gravity, wind and seismic analysis
  • Foundations: pads, rafts and piles
  • Connection and detail design
  • Assessment and strengthening of existing structures
  • Independent design checking

Works closely with

CivilElectricalMechanical

Electrical infrastructure

Power has to be generated, transformed and carried. These assets rely on civil and structural work as much as on electrical design.

Typical scope

  • Substation platforms, bunds and equipment foundations
  • Transmission tower and pole foundations
  • Solar and wind plant layouts and supports
  • Cable routes, trenches and ducts
  • Equipment supports and gantries
  • Coordination with electrical designers

Works closely with

CivilStructural

Mechanical plant

Machines move, vibrate and need to be maintained. The structures around them must allow for that.

Typical scope

  • Equipment foundations designed for dynamic loads
  • Turbine and generator halls
  • Pipe racks and pipe supports
  • Crane girders and runway beams
  • Plant platforms, stairs and access
  • Coordination with mechanical designers

Works closely with

StructuralCivil

Integrated delivery

Why one team matters

Most problems on site start at the boundaries between disciplines. Planning them together removes those gaps early.

Fewer clashes

One coordinated model

  • Foundations sized with the real equipment loads
  • Cable and pipe routes planned with the structure
  • Openings and embedments agreed before drawings issue

Faster decisions

One point of contact

  • Questions answered without passing between firms
  • Changes assessed across every discipline at once
  • Clear ownership of the design

Lower risk

Fewer surprises on site

  • Interfaces checked before construction
  • Less rework and fewer variations
  • Programme protected

The principal engineer

Design environments

Common standards in each market

Every country has its own codes. These are the standards typically used for structural design in each one.

India

South Asia

IS codes from the Bureau of Indian Standards, such as IS 456 for concrete and IS 800 for steel

UAE

Middle East

Local authority regulations, commonly alongside British, European and American design codes

Qatar

Middle East

Qatar Construction Specifications with British, European and American design codes

Australia

Oceania

Australian Standards, such as the AS/NZS 1170 loading series and AS 3600 for concrete

New Zealand

Oceania

New Zealand Standards, such as NZS 3101 for concrete, with AS/NZS 1170 for loads

USA

North America

American codes and standards such as ASCE 7 for loads, ACI 318 for concrete and AISC 360 for steel

Home/Process

Process

From first sketch to handover

Six stages, each with clear inputs, outputs and a check before moving on

Concept

Compare schemes for cost, span, speed and architectural fit

Analysis

Model gravity, wind and seismic actions and check stability

Design

Size members, connections and foundations to the governing code

Documentation

Drawings, schedules and specifications for tender and construction

Review

Independent checks of calculations and drawings before issue

Construction

Site queries answered, critical works inspected, changes approved

Stage by stage

What happens at each step

01

Concept

Explore structural schemes and pick the one that best balances cost, span, speed and architectural fit.

Inputs

  • Architectural concept
  • Site and ground information
  • Client brief and budget

Outputs

  • Scheme options with pros and cons
  • Preferred scheme
  • Early cost drivers
02

Analysis

Build the analysis model and check how the structure behaves under gravity, wind and seismic actions.

Inputs

  • Preferred scheme
  • Design codes and loads
  • Equipment and services loads

Outputs

  • Analysis model
  • Load paths confirmed
  • Stability and drift checks
03

Design

Size the members, connections and foundations to the governing code.

Inputs

  • Analysis results
  • Geotechnical report
  • Coordination input

Outputs

  • Design calculations
  • Member sizes
  • Foundation design
04

Documentation

Produce the drawings, schedules and specifications for tender and construction.

Inputs

  • Final design
  • Architectural and services drawings

Outputs

  • Drawing set
  • Schedules
  • Specifications
05

Review

Check calculations and drawings independently before anything is issued.

Inputs

  • Draft drawings
  • Design calculations

Outputs

  • Check comments closed out
  • Issued drawings
  • Design certificate where required
06

Construction

Support the contractor on site until handover.

Inputs

  • Contractor queries
  • Shop drawings
  • Site conditions

Outputs

  • Query responses
  • Shop drawing reviews
  • Site inspection reports
